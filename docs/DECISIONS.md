# Decisions and assumptions

Every material assumption, in the order they were made. The priority used when sources conflicted was: (1) the user's live instructions, (2) the implementation prompt, (3) the KPM game-plan PDF for curriculum and pedagogy, (4) the reference video for observed interaction mechanics only.

## What the reference video does and does not show

`example.mp4` is 14.6 s, 1920x1080, and starts mid-race. Observed: a five-lane race across the top of a large touch panel with one named racer per pupil; five equal answer panels below, each with a coloured header showing the equation, an answer field and a keypad (1-9, C, 0, Go); a winner overlay ("... MENANG!") with a "Main Lagi" button; children answering at the same time.

**Not observed, therefore decided here (not copied):** the setup flow, the countdown, what happens after a wrong answer, hints, pausing, results and any data handling. The characters in the video (ducks), their art, sounds and layout details were not reused; MathRacing uses original vector rocket-kart style vehicles, synthesised sounds and its own wording. No frames from the video are stored in the repository.

## Product decisions

1. **Wrong answers are private and gentle.** The item stays active, the keypad clears, a soft "Cuba lagi." appears in that pupil's panel only, and a low short sound plays. No wrong-answer count is shown to the class (plan section 8). _Amended by decision 30: in Quick Race the 3rd wrong answer to one question now moves the racer back a step._
2. **Hint after 2 wrong attempts, support after 3.** The hint is a small conceptual sentence per template. At 3 wrong attempts the pupil first gets a picture of the problem where the template has one (no change of difficulty); a second step offers, never forces, a "Soalan lebih mudah" (simpler related item, one level lower). Both are teacher-switchable, applied to every player under the same rule, disclosed on the Setup screen and recorded as `support_used` in the evidence. This is how the "no silent easier path" rule from the prompt is met.
3. **One difficulty level per race.** All players get the same template sequence (a shared blueprint) and the same level, with different values. Topics in a mixed race are rotated evenly. No speed bonus: every correct answer moves exactly one step.
4. **Same-frame finish tie-break.** After the race is finished, submissions are frozen. The only exception is a finishing answer whose own timestamp is earlier than the declared winner's: it merely arrived late in the event queue, so it wins and the earlier "winner" becomes runner-up. Exactly one winner is ever shown. An exact timestamp tie goes to the answer processed first.
5. **Practice mode** has no winner: each pupil completes the configured number of items and the race ends when everyone is done (or the teacher ends it). Cooperative mode is architected for (the engine takes a mode and a source of questions) but not built.
6. **Ready screen doubles as a touch check.** Each pupil touches a large button in their own panel to confirm; when all have, a 3-2-1 countdown starts. The teacher can also start immediately.
7. **Pause is press-and-hold** (0.6 s) on the DOM button above the track, or Esc, so a child cannot pause by a stray touch. Answers being typed are kept and paused time is not counted in response times. The race also pauses automatically when the tab is hidden.
8. **Results are class-safe.** The class sees finishing order and progress. Teacher evidence (attempts, hints, support, per-SK counts) is behind an explicit button and is labelled evidence, never a mark or Tahap Penguasaan.
9. **Main Lagi uses a new seed** (so the questions are new). If the teacher typed a seed, the replay uses `<seed>-<n>`, which is still reproducible.
10. **Litres are written "L"**, not "l", because a lowercase l is easily misread on a classroom screen.
11. **Number pad omits a separate backspace**: it has 0-9, C (clear) and Go/Hantar, matching the layout in the video. Build-value has an Undo.

## Curriculum decisions

12. **SP codes are not invented.** The plan lists SK ranges only. Templates carry the plan's SK range and an empty SP list; docs/CURRICULUM-MAPPING.md records this and the other gaps. Packs are `draft-pending-academic-review`.
13. **Bahasa Melayu is complete; English is a full UI translation** with per-key fallback to BM (never a raw key). English does not invent curriculum codes.
14. **Money is integer sen** everywhere; answers compare as exact decimal strings. Year 1 money uses 5/10/20/50 sen coins and RM1/RM5/RM10 notes; Year 2 up to RM100 uses whole-ringgit notes. Coins and notes are drawn as original abstract vector art.
15. **Year 2 times tables** are limited to 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 (and one-digit x 10). The plan's example RM48 / 6 is honoured for money division. To be confirmed by the reviewer.
16. **A remainder is asked as its own item** ("? baki 2" or "3 baki ?") because the keypad takes one number; every division template declares whether a remainder can occur.
17. **All five widgets are implemented** (number pad, choice tiles, tap picture, order strip, build value) with tap-based order/build (no drag needed). The plan's clock-picker and draw-shape widgets are not.

## Engineering decisions

18. **Stack** (all pinned in package-lock.json): Phaser 4.2.1, TypeScript 6.0.3, Vite 8.3.0, Vitest 5.0.1, Playwright 1.63.0, vite-plugin-pwa 1.3.0, ESLint 10 with typescript-eslint 8.70.1, Prettier 3.9.9. **TypeScript is 6.0.3, not the newer 7.x**, because typescript-eslint 8.70.1 declares `typescript >=4.8.4 <6.1.0`.
19. **Ten active pointers** are reserved (`input.activePointers: 10`), five for pupils and spares so accidental contacts cannot steal a slot.
20. **Pointer ownership** is pure logic (`InputOwnership`) behind a thin adapter (`PointerRouter`); a tap completes on pointer-up over the same control it started on inside its owner panel. Taps activate on release, not press, so a dragging finger never presses.
21. **Crisp rendering on scaled displays:** the canvas is sized in device pixels (capped at 2x) and shown at CSS size. Phaser's `zoom` did not do this in RESIZE mode (checked in a browser), so sizing is explicit.
22. **The Phaser engine is lazy-loaded** (dynamic import), so Setup opens from a 182 KB script and the 1.4 MB game chunk loads when a race starts. Both are precached by the service worker.
23. **Autosave after every attempt** (fire-and-forget to IndexedDB; a failure shows a warning toast rather than being swallowed). If IndexedDB is unavailable the app falls back to memory and says so.
24. **Settings never contain names.** localStorage holds only classroom-level choices; player names live in memory for the session (and are prefilled after "New Setup" while the page stays open).
25. **The end-to-end tests need no test hooks in the app.** Because sessions are seeded, the tests import the app's own pure curriculum/layout modules, compute the exact questions and control positions for a seed, and drive the real browser. Multi-touch tests use Chrome DevTools Protocol touch events.
26. **The `?diag` pointer overlay exists only in development** (`import.meta.env.DEV`).
27. **Git:** the repository was initialised after the first build. `example.mp4` is git-ignored because it shows real pupils' faces; `.gitattributes` keeps line endings consistent on Windows.
28. **The service worker cannot register in Claude's embedded browser pane** (a limitation of that pane); offline behaviour was verified in Playwright's Chromium instead.
29. **Frame errors must never freeze the screen.** Bug found in use: on a narrow or tall window with fractional display scaling (for example 1103x887 at 150%), the very large "Mula!" countdown label was wrapped into a box narrower than one letter, Phaser threw while drawing, and an uncaught exception in Phaser's loop stops the whole game, leaving the screen frozen on the countdown "1". Fixes: (a) text with only a width limit is now one line that shrinks to fit (only text with a height limit wraps); (b) `TextPool` catches Phaser's wrap error and shrinks or falls back to no wrap; (c) the countdown card and font size follow the track's width and height; (d) each scene frame runs inside `guardedFrame`, which logs the error and keeps the game running, and after 30 consecutive failing frames tells the app to stop the race and return to Setup with a message. Regression tests cover four awkward window shapes with every child confirming Ready by touch, and the abort-to-Setup path.

30. **Setback: the 3rd wrong answer moves a racer back one step (owner decision, reverses the original "never backwards" rule).** The project owner asked for cars to go backward after three wrong answers so the game is "different and unique", overriding the implementation brief and the KPM plan's "no negative distance" note. Because the track is shared and public, the rule is bounded and switchable: **on by default** with a Setup checkbox; **Quick Race only** (Practice never sets a racer back); **once per question** (further wrong answers to the same question cost nothing more, so a stuck pupil cannot spiral); **one step**; **never below the start line**; the hint (after 2 wrong) and support (after 3) still work unchanged; the visuals and sound are neutral (a soft grey dust puff, two low descending notes, an amber-tinted private message; no red, no names, no "wrong" counter on the track); and the class-facing results never mention it. The teacher evidence view has a "Setbacks" column and the CSV has `step_delta` and `progress_after`. Every player follows the same rule (the toggle is per race, not per pupil). Reviewer note: this is a classroom-culture call, so confirm it suits your classes; switch it off in Setup if not.
31. **Question index is decoupled from progress.** Progress can now go down, so the next question comes from a per-player `itemsDealt` counter, not from `progress`; a racer who is set back is dealt a _new_ question, never one already solved. The blueprint repeats its templates past `raceLength`, and Setup pre-validates two rounds of items per player.
32. **Movement animation.** Each racer has a `CarMotion` (`src/game/ui/motion.ts`): a critically damped spring using the exact closed-form solution, so it is identical at 30, 60 and 144 fps and after a stalled frame, and retargeting mid-move never jumps. Forward is quicker with a nose-up lean, exhaust puffs, speed lines and a longer rocket flame; backward is slower and softer, keeps facing forward ("rolling back"), wobbles briefly and leaves neutral dust. Wheels turn with the distance actually rolled. Reduced motion removes all of it (instant move, no particles) and shows a 320 ms static ring so the change is still noticeable. Particles live in a fixed pool.
33. **Animation is on by default; the device's reduced-motion flag is not inherited.** The game runs on Android tablets and TV smartboards, where the OS "remove animations" switch is often on for battery or kiosk reasons and says nothing about the pupils in the room. Inheriting it silently turned the car animation off (found on a Windows PC with animation effects disabled). "Kurangkan gerakan" in Setup is now the only switch, off by default, and a teacher's saved choice is kept. Settings saved by earlier builds have that flag reset once (a stored `revision` marks the new format); a choice saved since is kept. The CSS `prefers-reduced-motion` rule for DOM transitions is unchanged.
34. **Track marks are car positions; track margins follow the lane height.** The start line, step marks and finish chequer used to be placed by width percentages while the cars also grew with the lane height, so on large 2-player screens a car stopped up to 150 px past its mark and the start line crossed the lane badges. `game/ui/trackGeometry.ts` now computes everything from one set of numbers: the car's nose is the reference point (at step 0 it touches the start line with the whole car waiting behind it, at each step it lands on that step's mark, at the last step it touches the chequer), and the badge, gaps and chequer width come from the lane height with pixel limits. Unit tests cover tablet, smartboard and desktop sizes for 2-5 players and every race length; an e2e test checks the nose against mark 1 at 2560x1440. Plan: `docs/PLAN-dynamic-track-lines.md`.
35. **Splash, main menu and a four-step setup; teacher options on their own screen.** The app opens with a splash that is inline in `index.html` (so it shows before any script and offline), preloads the game engine, and moves on to the menu by itself after at least 1.5 s (6 s cap; a tap only makes it quicker; once per tab). The menu has two large cards (Main = Lumba Pantas, Latihan), "Main semula" (repeat the last setup, shown once a setup exists) and a small "Tetapan guru" corner. Setup is four touch-first steps (Tahun, Topik & Tahap, Pemain, Sedia?) that each fit 1024x600 without scrolling; the stepper jumps to any step. Sound, reduce motion, hints, support, setback, the session seed, local data and the PBD notice moved to the teacher screen, with no lock (owner decision). Vehicles stay fixed per slot (colour + symbol + number rule). Splash, menu and settings are app screens inside the `SETUP` phase; the race state machine is unchanged. The page-wide CSS no longer follows the OS `prefers-reduced-motion` flag either: the teacher's setting (a `reduce-motion` class on `<html>`) is the one switch, extending #33. Plan: `docs/PLAN-splash-menu-setup.md`.
36. **Cruise movement: cars drive on their own; answers change speed, not position (owner decision; amends #3, #4 and #30).** The project owner asked for every car to move towards the finish at the same steady pace, a correct answer to give a boost and a wrong answer to slow the car down. It is **on by default** (a teacher-screen toggle, "Kenderaan bergerak sendiri", switches back to the step race) and applies to **Quick Race and Practice** (Practice has no winner and ends when every car has arrived). Numbers (`core/cruise.ts`, `DEFAULT_CRUISE`, tune after a classroom try): one step per 15 s with no answers; a correct answer adds exactly **one step** of extra distance over 1.2 s (boosts add up), so every correct answer is still worth the same (#3); a wrong answer drops the car to 30% speed for 2.5 s, restarting (not stacking) on another wrong answer, and a correct answer cancels it. A car **never moves back** in cruise: the **setback (#30) belongs to the step race only**, and its option is shown only when cruise is off. A car may reach the finish on cruise alone (owner: fine, answering is the only way to go faster); with these numbers a wrong answer costs about 0.12 steps against one step for a correct one, so doing nothing never beats trying. The race always ends. **Finishing happens in `tick`, not `submit`**, from exact crossing times (speed is piecewise constant): the earliest crossing wins even inside one frame; crossings within 1 µs are a tie, broken by more correct answers, then the earlier last correct answer, then slot order. This replaces the late-submit rule (#4) in cruise. Evidence: `stepDelta` is +1 for a correct answer; `progressAfter` is the car's distance (2 decimals) at the attempt; a new `speedEffect` field and CSV column `speed_effect` (`boost`/`slow`/`none`); `SessionMeta.movement`. Reduced motion still moves the cars (their position is information) with a static ring on each boost or slow-down. Plan: `docs/PLAN-cruise-boost.md`.
37. **Idle crawl: a car whose pupil stops answering only crawls (owner decision; amends #36).** The owner asked for cars to keep moving but very slowly when pupils idle, so not answering is never a strategy. Per car: after **10 s without a submitted answer** the car drops to **20% of the cruise pace** (one step every 75 s) until the pupil answers again; any answer, right or wrong, or asking for a simpler item ends the crawl (touches without submitting do not). A wrong answer applies the normal slow-down and then returns to full pace, not the crawl, so trying always beats waiting. When every pupil idles, every car crawls. A pause never counts as idling. With these numbers a pupil who never answers needs about 9.5 minutes for an 8-step race (was 2 minutes). The crawling pupil sees a private, friendly "Jawab untuk memecut!" in their own panel (a hint on screen is kept instead); nothing appears on the shared track except the slow car, which has no flame, puffs or speed lines. No evidence fields change. Plan: `docs/PLAN-idle-crawl.md`.
38. **Android APK through Capacitor 6.** For testing on Android tablets and smartboards the web build is wrapped in a native shell with Capacitor 6.2.2 (`android/`, `capacitor.config.json`, `npm run apk` in `scripts/build-apk.mjs`). The whole web build is copied into the APK, so it is offline from the first launch; nothing is sent anywhere (#hard rules unchanged). Capacitor 6 rather than 8: it targets SDK 34 with JDK 17, which is what the build PC has; Capacitor 8 needs SDK 36 and JDK 21. `npm audit` reports `tar` inside `@capacitor/cli`: that tool only runs on the build PC (copying files into `android/`) and is not in the APK; revisit when moving to Capacitor 8. The activity is `sensorLandscape` (same as the PWA manifest) and full screen; the launch screen is the splash navy with no Capacitor branding; the launcher icon is `public/icons/icon-192.png`. Inside the native app the service worker is **not** registered (`window.Capacitor.isNativePlatform()`): the files are already local, and a cached worker could serve the previous version after an APK update. The APK is debug-signed for testing; a release keystore (for Play Store or managed installs) must be created and kept by the owner, never committed.
39. **Pupils choose their racer: five vehicles or five animals (owner decision; amends #35 "vehicles stay fixed per slot").** The owner asked for pupils to pick what runs to the finish, including animals such as a duck or goose. On the Pemain step each player card's racer picture opens a sheet with **Kenderaan** (roket, kart, kereta laju, piring terbang, van) and **Haiwan** (itik, angsa, ayam, penyu, arnab). **Colour, symbol and lane number stay tied to the slot** (the accessibility cue and the link to the pupil's panel); only the shape is a choice, and until a pupil picks, each slot has its old vehicle. **Two active players never share a racer:** a racer held by another active player is greyed out and shows that player's badge; saved choices that clash are resolved in slot order (the later seat gets its default, or the first free racer). **Animals use natural colours** (yellow duck, white goose, brown chicken, green turtle, grey rabbit) with dark outlines so they stay visible on the grey road; the player's colour is on a scarf (a saddle cloth on the turtle) and a ringed badge, so the colour cue stays on the track. Every racer is one list of pure parts (`game/ui/racerShapes.ts`) drawn by both the track (Phaser) and the DOM screens (SVG), which also removed the duplicated vehicle drawings; the body stays inside the car's reach so the track marks (#34) line up for every racer. Animals use the same motion data as cars: legs step with the distance travelled (frame-rate independent), a boost flaps the wings (ears back on the rabbit, head out on the turtle) with speed lines and dust instead of exhaust, a slow-down drops the head, and a crawl is a slow waddle. The name chip is lifted above tall animals. **Saved on the device:** `Settings.racers` keeps one racer per seat (Pemain 1-5) in localStorage; it is never linked to a name, so names are still never persisted. **Recorded:** `SessionMeta.playerRacers` and a CSV `racer` column (the stable id, e.g. `duck`; empty for older sessions); results and the teacher evidence show each pupil's racer. No new sounds. Plan: `docs/PLAN-racer-choice.md`.
